## Editorial anchors

- **Seeing Jesus:** An encounter is interpreted through the figure’s words/presence, the dreamer’s faith, and the emotional after-effect—not as automatic revelation.
- **Water rising:** Rising water shows pressure crossing a boundary; speed, source, exits, and response reveal accumulation versus escalation and agency versus paralysis.
- **Seeing your future self:** The future self is a projected conversation with possible identity; resemblance, change, message, and emotional distance show hope or fear about becoming.
- **Confessing your love:** The confession rehearses disclosure; person, risk, and response distinguish attraction from recognition, apology, courage, or another truth.
- **Hair falling out:** Visible, difficult-to-conceal change is the anchor; rate, audience, sensation, and reaction distinguish appearance anxiety from stress or health worry.
- **Water:** Meaning comes from movement × condition × position: water can sustain, reveal, restrict, or overwhelm depending on depth, clarity, temperature, force, and response.
- **Dog bite:** The bite is a rupture in expected safety; relationship, warning, location, injury, and response distinguish betrayal from boundary correction or protection.
- **Cheating on your partner:** The gap between behavior and emotional need—guilt, excitement, secrecy, tenderness, or indifference—reveals the possible conflict without proving literal betrayal.

## Category-specific findings

- **Biblical:** Seeing Jesus needed an explicit boundary between a faith-based reading and claims of revelation. The strongest repair was pairing spiritual language with Scripture-in-context, wise counsel, and observable circumstances.
- **Water:** Water pages need a variable framework (movement, condition, depth, position) and a visible distinction between broad water, rising water, flood, ocean, and other specialized pages. “Water means emotion” is not sufficient.
- **Body-related:** Hair falling out must keep bodily sensation and health anxiety available without diagnosing. The image is most useful as visible change that may be difficult to conceal.
- **Objects/relationships controls:** Dog bite and cheating show that a page can be structurally rich yet still generic if the inherited catalogue outruns the anchor. The repair must reduce lists, not add more.

## Semantic-neighbor findings

- `seeing-jesus` / `speaking-to-jesus`: seeing centers presence and discernment; speaking centers the conversation.
- `water-rising` / `water`: rising water centers pressure crossing a boundary; water provides the full condition-and-position framework.
- `seeing-your-future-self` / `the-future`: future-self centers projected identity; the-future centers uncertainty about what lies ahead.
- `confessing-your-love-to-someone` / `someone-confessing-their-love-to-you`: one page rehearses disclosure; the other examines being chosen or receiving a confession.
- `hair-falling-out` / `hair-growing-rapidly`: involuntary visible loss contrasts with accelerated growth and changing self-image.
- `dog-bite` / `dog`: the bite is a rupture event; dog is the broader relationship with loyalty, protection, companionship, and instinct.
- `cheating-on-your-partner` / `partner-cheating-on-you`: the pages should keep the direction of trust anxiety explicit—fear of betraying versus fear of being betrayed.

## What improved the reader experience

1. Putting the anchor in the answer paragraph made the first screen useful without a scroll through folklore or keyword lists.
2. Replacing association lists with detail → response → possible meaning reasoning made scenarios feel interpretive rather than encyclopedic.
3. Trust boundaries worked best when attached to the natural fear of the page: revelation, prediction, medical diagnosis, betrayal, or spiritual warning.
4. Specific questions (what crossed the boundary, who noticed, what was protected, what the future self did) invite interpretation rather than data collection.

## What did not improve enough

1. Inherited generic tails still reduce automated distinctiveness, especially on Water, Dog bite, Hair falling out, and Cheating.
2. High-neighbor pages need visible “this page focuses on / the neighbor focuses on” language earlier than the related-dream cards.
3. Short pages such as Seeing your future self can be useful, but need enough scenario reasoning to avoid looking like a generic future-symbol definition.

## Standard revision

The four Phase 3.5 gates remain correct. Add one operational rule:

> **Inherited-content review must be performed on the rendered route, not only on the enrichment object.** Mark each inherited section Keep, Adapt, Replace, or Remove, then re-read the first 40% after composition. A page does not pass because a strong override exists if the renderer still places generic base prose before or after it.

Also add a **direction-of-trust rule** for relationship neighbors: when two pages reverse the subject/object of a fear (confessing vs receiving, cheating vs being cheated on), the distinction must appear in the opening or first scenario, not only in metadata.

## P1 calibration decision

The methodology is **ready to continue**, with the inherited-content and neighbor-visibility rules now treated as hard gates. The batch confirms that shorter, more specific pages outperform longer catalogues for human readers. Continue with the next P1 batch only after the remaining generic rendered tails on the current eight pages are cleaned up where practical; do not use the unchanged-score pages as evidence that the repairs failed.
