Scores are triage signals. The anchor and the reader-test evidence are the approval standard.

## Most common failure patterns

1. The opening named a broad symbol but did not answer the exact experience.
2. Emotional, symbolic, spiritual, and practical sections repeated “stress, healing, uncertainty, and growth” with different headings.
3. Scenario lists existed but did not explain why behavior, setting, or outcome changed the meaning.
4. Reflection questions collected details without helping the reader reason from those details.
5. Related links shared keywords but did not state the meaningful relationship or boundary between pages.
6. Broad hub pages blurred into action-specific neighbors.
7. High-risk subjects—illness, death, omens, numbers, and ex-partners—needed an explicit distinction between tradition, personal possibility, and literal prediction.
8. Structured scenario labels leaked into audit evidence, making good reasoning look less human than it was.

## Most effective repair techniques

- Start with the reader's actual tension, not the symbol's dictionary definition.
- Name the variable that changes meaning: destination, failure point, care, gaze direction, request, warning time, or public/private identity.
- Keep the strongest existing opening when it already recognizes the scene; repair the missing lenses around it.
- Give each page one sentence that states its independent interpretive anchor.
- Use conditional scenarios tied to action and outcome rather than adding a universal list.
- Let practical interpretation distinguish missing information, avoidance, capacity, support, evidence, and choice.
- Make related links explain why the neighboring page is useful and where its subject differs.
- Treat spiritual and biblical material as reflective frameworks, not evidence of prediction.
- Add safety language only where the subject naturally invites literal fear.
- Preserve ordinary explanations such as recent media, bodily sensation, travel, or memory processing.

## Editorial-standard additions

The standard should now require an explicit “anchor sentence” during drafting: “This page's unique interpretive anchor is…” The sentence should identify a relationship, process, or contextual distinction that a nearby page cannot absorb.

The standard should also require a “chain-of-meaning” check for scenario pages:

**Image or event → context → emotion or response → possible meaning → waking-life question.**

For high-risk imagery, add a three-way check:

**Traditional association → personal possibility → what the dream cannot prove.**

## Patterns not to repeat

- Do not turn every page into a long four-lens essay.
- Do not use “stress, healing, growth, and uncertainty” as interchangeable filler.
- Do not treat a selected symbol as a diagnosis, omen, prophecy, or instruction to act.
- Do not use an ex dream as evidence of another person's thoughts or future contact.
- Do not use a related link merely because a keyword overlaps.
- Do not let audit thresholds dictate awkward wording or unnecessary scenarios.
- Do not merge a broad page with an action-specific page unless the search intent and independent value are genuinely indistinguishable.

## Three before/after examples

### Being Lost

**Before:** “Dreams about being lost often reflect uncertainty, confusion, lack of direction, life transitions, self-discovery…”

**After:** “Being lost in a dream often means that something important still matters to you, but the route toward it feels unclear.”

The repair gives the reader a recognizable tension—destination versus route—and then develops setting, help, shame, urgency, and outcome without claiming that all lost dreams mean confusion.

### Unable to Call or Communicate

**Before:** The page described no signal, failed messages, and emotional distance, but the audit could not identify a distinct interpretive insight.

**After:** “Being unable to communicate is not one failure with one meaning. The exact point where connection breaks provides the clearest clue.”

The repair separates access, expression, delivery, listening, trust, and protective silence, while preserving the page's concrete phone imagery.

### 11:11

**Before:** The page used synchronicity, alignment, manifestation, and spiritual awakening as broad meanings.

**After:** “11:11 in a dream may feel meaningful because repeating numbers naturally catch attention. Its interpretation depends on what the number meant to you…not on a guaranteed message.”

The repair keeps spiritual traditions available while clearly separating them from established fact, personal association, and ordinary pattern recognition.

## Pages that remain questionable

Passing P0 does not make these finished model pages. They remain the next review candidates:

- **11:11 (P1):** safer and more reflective, but still broad and close to Angel Numbers.
- **Crows (P2):** the attention-director distinction is sound, but several spiritual associations remain culturally broad.
- **Tsunami (P2):** the scale/preparation distinction works; Flood remains a useful neighbor to monitor.
- **Marrying a Celebrity (P2):** idealization and self-worth are distinct, but the page should be checked against Marrying a Crush as the library grows.
- **Eyes (P2):** the broad page remains semantically close to Eyes Staring at You in the Dark; its direction-of-attention distinction must stay visible.
- **Saying No (P2):** it remains close to Saying No at a Wedding because the request-specific pages share boundary language; the broader page should keep its consent-and-consequence focus.

## Process decision

The P0 methodology is ready to be refined for P1, but the next step should be a human review of these questionable P2/P1 pages—not an automatic expansion of all 51 current P1 pages. The anchor-first brief should remain mandatory, and the next batch should again be small enough to compare against its semantic neighbors before wider application.
