## Methodology findings

### What worked

- Relationship/process anchors were stronger than theme words: structure failure, discovery-before-duty, cumulative consumption, reduced agency, observer distance, maintained separation, external counterpart, and lost spatial capacity.
- Replacing legacy content produced clearer pages than appending to it.
- Trust boundaries worked best inside the interpretation: no prediction, diagnosis, moral verdict, supernatural proof, or compulsory action.
- Neighbor links became useful when they named the changed reader problem.

### What did not

- Automated scoring penalized several concise replacements and still called clear distinctions generic. Tier movement is therefore not the success measure.
- The audit's “practical” and “symbolic” detectors favor length and certain vocabulary over a short usable decision.
- Exact-duplication checks cannot validate conceptual distinction; the pair reviews remain necessary.

### Recurring patterns

- **Generic legacy:** isolated words, “often symbolizes” lists, broad growth/healing endings, and repeated emotional labels.
- **Conditional weakness:** old scenarios named a variant but did not explain why response or outcome changed meaning.
- **Water:** contact, containment, visibility, and movement provide stronger distinctions than “emotion.”
- **Spiritual/biblical:** tradition must be separated from certainty and personal context.
- **Sensitive intimacy/body:** consent, agency, ordinary causes, and nonliteral framing must appear early.
- **Semantic neighbors:** paired pages remain viable only when the difference is a different reader question, not vocabulary.
