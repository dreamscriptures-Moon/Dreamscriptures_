# Phase 4 — P1 batch 1 selection and reader-first diagnosis

| Requirement | Pages |
|---|---|
| Weakest categories (Biblical, Water) | `seeing-jesus`, `water-rising` |
| High semantic-neighbor risk | `seeing-your-future-self`, `confessing-your-love-to-someone` |

## Reader-first repair briefs

### seeing-jesus

- **Reader wants:** reassurance about whether a vivid Jesus encounter is spiritual, emotional, or both.
- **Current recognition:** the page says Jesus dreams can feel significant even for people who are not deeply religious.
- **Failure:** the opening is a list of faith keywords; the rendered “A closer look” contains placeholder-like fragments (“Different. Important.”).
- **Anchor:** An encounter with Jesus is interpreted through the relationship between the figure’s words/presence, the dreamer’s faith, and the emotional after-effect—not as automatic revelation.
- **Inherited audit:** opening **Replace**; placeholder “A closer look” **Remove**; any Scripture section **Adapt** to distinguish text from personal discernment; questions and related links **Keep/Adapt**.

### water-rising

- **Reader wants:** to know whether rising water reflects emotional overload, danger, or an approaching change.
- **Current recognition:** the page mentions mounting pressure but does not explain rate, source, exits, or response in the first screen.
- **Failure:** inherited prose reduces water to feelings and subconscious symbolism.
- **Anchor:** Rising water shows pressure crossing a boundary; speed, source, available exits, and the dreamer’s response reveal accumulation versus sudden escalation and agency versus paralysis.
- **Inherited audit:** generic water opener **Replace**; rising-water context and examples **Keep/Adapt**; broad water taxonomy **Remove**.

### seeing-your-future-self

- **Reader wants:** to understand whether the older/future self is a prediction, warning, hope, or unresolved identity question.
- **Current recognition:** the current page already says the figure gives expectation a face and is not dependable foreknowledge.
- **Failure:** the page is short and needs stronger detail-reasoning around the future self’s behavior and the dreamer’s response.
- **Anchor:** The future self is a projected conversation with one’s possible identity; resemblance, change, message, and emotional distance show what the dreamer hopes to grow into or fears becoming.
- **Inherited audit:** first screen **Keep/Adapt**; scenario section **Adapt**; generic future symbolism **Replace**; prediction disclaimer **Keep**.

### confessing-your-love-to-someone

- **Reader wants:** to know whether the dream reveals literal romantic desire or a need to say something emotionally true.
- **Current recognition:** “what finally gets said” recognizes the tension before confession.
- **Failure:** the rendered opening trails into generic emotional keywords and does not distinguish person, audience, response, or relief.
- **Anchor:** The confession is a rehearsal of disclosure; who is told, what is risked, and how the listener responds reveal whether the dream concerns attraction, recognition, apology, courage, or a truth unrelated to romance.
- **Inherited audit:** keyword-heavy opener **Replace**; confession tension **Keep/Adapt**; generic vulnerability lists **Remove**; neighbor link to reciprocal confession **Keep with visible distinction**.

### hair-falling-out

- **Reader wants:** reassurance and a specific explanation for a frightening loss-of-appearance dream, without a medical prediction.
- **Current recognition:** the page now notes visibility and real physical triggers.
- **Failure:** the inherited “hair means identity/confidence” passage is broad; the first screen needs the visible-loss anchor and emotional alternatives.
- **Anchor:** Hair falling out dramatizes change that feels visible and difficult to conceal; speed, audience, bodily sensation, and attempts to hide it distinguish appearance anxiety from loss of control or health worry.
- **Inherited audit:** opening **Replace**; physical-trigger note **Keep**; generic identity list **Adapt**; scenarios/questions **Keep/Adapt**.

### water

- **Reader wants:** a usable way to interpret a broad water dream without being told that all water equals emotion.
- **Current recognition:** the opening already leads with movement and the dreamer’s position.
- **Failure:** the page is strong but can make its detail chain more explicit and distinguish water from ocean/flood neighbors earlier.
- **Anchor:** Water meaning comes from movement × condition × the dreamer’s position: the same element can sustain, reveal, restrict, or overwhelm depending on depth, clarity, temperature, force, and response.
- **Inherited audit:** current core **Keep**; broad “water is emotion” remnants **Replace**; practical examples and personal-history caveat **Keep**; neighbor copy **Adapt**.

### dog-bite

- **Reader wants:** to understand why a bite from a usually trusted animal feels personal and whether it implies betrayal.
- **Current recognition:** the rendered page says, “A dog bite feels personal.”
- **Failure:** the inherited section quickly turns that feeling into a betrayal list and compares snakes before explaining trust, warning, and boundary context.
- **Anchor:** A dog bite is a rupture in expected safety; the dog’s relationship to the dreamer, warning signs, location, and injury show whether the dream concerns betrayal, boundary correction, guilt, or protective instinct.
- **Inherited audit:** first screen **Keep/Adapt**; generic betrayal list **Replace**; snake comparison **Move to contextual neighbor link**; scenarios **Adapt**.

### cheating-on-your-partner

- **Reader wants:** to know whether the dream means they want to cheat or whether it reflects a current relationship tension.
- **Current recognition:** the page acknowledges that the dream can feel guilty, freeing, or emotionally real.
- **Failure:** the rendered opening repeats “emotional” many times and offers a catalogue rather than a decision path.
- **Anchor:** The dream’s meaning turns on the gap between behavior and emotional need: guilt, excitement, secrecy, tenderness, or indifference reveal whether the scene rehearses conflict, novelty, unmet connection, identity, or ordinary memory processing.
- **Inherited audit:** repetitive emotional catalogue **Replace**; emotion-based distinctions **Keep/Adapt**; literal-betrayal reassurance **Keep**; related links **Adapt** to explain next-step relevance.

## Common diagnosis

The first batch confirms that P1 work should prioritize replacing inherited generic prose, not adding more sections. The strongest existing pages already contain usable material; the repair is to move the anchor into the first screen and turn scenario lists into detail → response → possible meaning reasoning.
