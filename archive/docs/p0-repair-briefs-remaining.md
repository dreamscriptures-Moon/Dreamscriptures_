# P0 editorial repair briefs — Remaining pages

## Relationship and ex-related batch

### Arguing With Your Ex

**Primary search intent:** Understand why conflict with a former partner returned and whether it represents unresolved anger, a repeated relationship pattern, self-criticism, or a need to be heard.

**What already works:** The page states that the dream does not necessarily mean the dreamer wants the relationship back. It notices yelling, explaining, walking away, calm disagreement, and an argument that never ends.

**Where it fails:** The interpretation repeats unresolved emotion without identifying what the argument is doing. Emotional, symbolic, spiritual, and practical sections largely rename the same idea.

**Closest neighbor:** `partner-cheating-on-you` in the audit; `ex-partner` is the closest editorial parent.

**Meaningful distinction:** A general ex dream asks what the person or period represents. This page asks what claim, wound, accusation, boundary, or version of events the dreamer is still contesting.

**Sections to repair:** Opening, description, four lenses, scenarios, reflection questions, metadata, and related links.

**Sections to preserve:** The non-reconciliation warning and the behavioral distinctions between explaining, yelling, leaving, and remaining calm.

**This page's unique interpretive anchor is…** the argument reveals what the dreamer is still trying to defend, correct, refuse, or have acknowledged—not simply whether feelings for the ex remain.

### Ex Texting You

**Primary search intent:** Understand why a message from an ex appeared, what the message and response mean, and whether the dream suggests actual contact.

**What already works:** The restrained opening is human and recognizes neutrality, curiosity, distance, and the idea that “not everything that returns is asking to come back.” The symbolic section already notices unread, deleted, answered, and unanswered messages.

**Where it fails:** The page has atmosphere but not enough causal reasoning. It needs to distinguish the sender, message content, response, expectation, and emotional aftermath.

**Closest neighbor:** `reliving-a-specific-memory`.

**Meaningful distinction:** A memory dream replays an event. A text dream creates a new, contained contact and lets the mind test whether the dreamer would open, answer, ignore, delete, or wait.

**Sections to repair:** Emotional, symbolic, practical, scenarios, questions, metadata, and contextual links.

**Sections to preserve:** The opening, quiet tone, distance metaphor, closure language, and warning against assuming return.

**This page's unique interpretive anchor is…** the message creates enough distance to rehearse a boundary, response, or imagined closure without requiring the relationship—or actual contact—to return.

### Ex-Partner

**Primary search intent:** Understand why an ex appeared at all and whether the dream means lingering love, reconciliation, memory processing, comparison, or personal change.

**What already works:** The page directly rejects mind-reading and reconciliation predictions. It contains useful distinctions among nostalgia, sadness, anger, confusion, peace, familiarity, and health.

**Where it fails:** The broad page repeats emotional processing, healing, lessons, and growth at length. Its independent role becomes blurred beside action-specific ex pages such as arguing, texting, or marrying an ex.

**Closest neighbor:** `marrying-an-ex`.

**Meaningful distinction:** Marrying an ex concerns recommitment, binding oneself again, or integrating something from the former relationship. This page is the broad interpretive hub for what the person and that life period represent before any particular action is considered.

**Sections to repair:** Opening, core description, symbolic and practical sections, contextual scenarios, reflection questions, and related links.

**Sections to preserve:** Emotional nuance, spiritual restraint, biblical refusal to treat the dream as an instruction, and warnings against nostalgia-driven action.

**This page's unique interpretive anchor is…** an ex can function as an emotional timestamp, allowing the dreamer to compare who they were in that relationship with a present need, pattern, fear, or stage of growth.

The page needed a more direct answer, clearer separation from Driving a Car Without Control, and questions about passengers, destination, pace, visibility, and stopping. Existing agency and direction material was retained.

## Fear, threat, and distress batch

### Being Sick

**Primary search intent:** Understand whether the dream predicts illness and why the dreamer felt weak, diagnosed, contagious, uncared for, or unable to continue normally.

**What already works:** The page clearly says a dream cannot diagnose illness and that real symptoms deserve medical attention. It recognizes reduced capacity, caregiving, actual bodily cues, and recovery.

**Where it fails:** Most sections collapse into “stress, burnout, imbalance, or healing.” The illness details, response to care, and social meaning of being unable to perform are underused.

**Closest neighbor:** `abandoned-baby` algorithmically; its real editorial neighbors are health anxiety, exhaustion, and recovery pages.

**Meaningful distinction:** This page concerns the loss of normal capacity and the response it permits or forces—rest, dependence, disclosure, treatment, or relief from performance.

**Sections to repair:** Opening, description, emotional, symbolic, practical, scenarios, questions, metadata, and related links.

**Sections to preserve:** Medical caution, body-cue example, reduced-capacity insight, and the distinction between sickness and recovery.

**This page's unique interpretive anchor is…** illness changes what the dreamer is able or expected to do, revealing their relationship with limitation, care, dependence, and permission to stop performing normally.

### Cemetery

**Primary search intent:** Understand whether a cemetery dream predicts death and why the place felt peaceful, frightening, neglected, familiar, or difficult to leave.

**What already works:** The page rejects death prediction, distinguishes peaceful and neglected cemeteries, includes Christian hope without certainty, and asks what past chapter deserves honor rather than continued carrying.

**Where it fails:** The repeated “ending, healing, closure, transformation” language overlooks what cemeteries uniquely do: mark, locate, revisit, and preserve the names of what has ended.

**Closest neighbor:** `marrying-an-ex` algorithmically; meaningful neighbors include Burial, Death, and Coffin.

**Meaningful distinction:** Burial centers the act of laying something down; a coffin centers containment and finality; a cemetery centers the ongoing relationship between the living and what has already been placed in the past.

**Sections to repair:** Opening, description, four lenses, scenarios, questions, metadata, and related links.

**Sections to preserve:** Non-predictive opening, biblical hope, peaceful-versus-dark distinction, and remembrance theme.

**This page's unique interpretive anchor is…** a cemetery organizes what has ended into a place that can be revisited, revealing whether the dreamer is honoring the past, tending neglected grief, searching for a specific memory, or struggling to leave.

### Crows

**Primary search intent:** Understand whether crows are a bad omen and why one crow, a flock, a watching bird, noise, flight, or entry into the home felt significant.

**What already works:** The page explicitly distinguishes warning from awareness and recognizes curiosity, calmness, unease, number, and behavior.

**Where it fails:** “Awareness, intuition, mystery, and transformation” remain too broad. The page does not explain that the crow directs attention but does not tell the dreamer what conclusion to reach.

**Closest neighbor:** `angel-numbers` algorithmically; meaningful neighbors are Owl, Being Watched, and dark-bird imagery.

**Meaningful distinction:** Unlike a generic warning, the crow's role is observational: following, calling, gathering, stealing, entering, or simply watching changes what kind of attention the dream explores.

**Sections to repair:** Opening, symbolic, spiritual, practical, scenarios, questions, metadata, and related links.

**Sections to preserve:** Warning-versus-awareness insight, emotional range, and rejection of automatic fear.

**This page's unique interpretive anchor is…** the crow acts as an attention director rather than an omen; its behavior and the dreamer's response show what kind of observation, social signal, adaptability, or unresolved concern is active.

### Eyes

**Primary search intent:** Understand what unusual, injured, closed, watching, glowing, or disembodied eyes mean and whether the dream represents insight, judgment, or a supernatural warning.

**What already works:** The page distinguishes seeing from avoiding, observation from judgment, clarity from discomfort, and several specific eye conditions.

**Where it fails:** It repeats awareness and truth without separating the person who sees, the object of attention, and the experience of being seen. It lacks questions and collides heavily with Eyes Staring at You in the Dark.

**Closest neighbor:** `eyes-staring-at-you-in-the-dark`.

**Meaningful distinction:** The neighbor centers an unseen watcher and fear in darkness. This page should interpret eyes broadly through ownership, condition, gaze direction, vision, recognition, and reciprocal attention.

**Sections to repair:** Opening, description, four lenses, scenarios, questions, metadata, and contextual links.

**Sections to preserve:** Seeing-versus-avoiding contradiction, injured/closed/many-eye variations, and the idea that feeling watched can involve self-consciousness rather than actual surveillance.

**This page's unique interpretive anchor is…** eye dreams change according to the direction of attention—seeing, refusing to see, looking at yourself, or being looked at—and each position creates a different question about perception, exposure, and judgment.
